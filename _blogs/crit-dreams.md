I have been riding bikes since I was very young, my dad is an avid cyclist. So after a year or so of not being able to rock climb due to injury I picked up cycling myself. 

If you are american, bike racing is pretty foreign (no pun intended). Europe dominates the cycling scene when it comes to the pro circuit, except in one area. That is american criterium racing.
Criterium, or crit for short, consists of a large group of riders on a closed loop racing as fast as possible for about an hour. In my opinion it is the most exciting form of bike racing to watch and I was very interested in trying it out for myself.

## The Art of Racing

Since crit races are very short in the context of most bike races, they are full gas. Expect to be pushing your FTP the second the race starts. Bike handling is a huge deal in these races, being able to corner at high speed can mean the difference between hanging with the front group and ending up on the ground with a broken collar bone. Bike handling isn't just for going fast though, a huge factor in crit racing is conserving energy. All being the same if two riders are at the end of the race together, the rider who has more energy left will win.

As someone looking into racing myself I thought I needed a level of strength that I didn't have so I started training, which I will talk about more later. Turns out that while this helps a lot, to start racing, and figure out how to handle a bit and ride better in a group you do not need to be the strongest person on the course. If you are strategic and know the fundamentals of racing a bike you can use a lot less strength to keep up with the rest of the group.
